▼What a unique and beautiful anime! - Tenmaku no Jaadugar (anime) - 9/13/26
Jaadugar: A Witch in Mongolia is one of the most unique anime that I have seen come out in a while, and one of the best. From the first episode I was entranced by the beautiful art style and presentation of culture. In fact, I think it is the presentation of culture that makes this anime so good. In a time where there is much intolerance towards Islam, it showed the culture and beliefs of Islam during that time period in an unbiased way. I can't speak well to the accuracy of what was shown because I have not personally studied Islam, but I am sure it was done well. For whatever fault their culture may have had, the beauty of humanity still came through. Because human culture is indeed beautiful for all of its faults. And so is the religion of Islam. Later on in the series, there is much of the Mongolian culture that is displayed. I am now curious what their traditional beliefs were or are, as what I saw I also thought was beautiful and full of spirit.

The series introduces us to a family that owns slaves, yet treats them well. Our main character is in fact one of their slaves. The show makes you feel conflicted in wanting to like the family that takes Sitara in. On one side they are slave owners, which is something that hopefully humanity knows now to be inexcusable. Yet on the other side, I was drawn as the viewer to actually like this family for their pursuit of genuine wisdom and how they treated the slaves so well, almost as a part of the family.
I think that it is to be understood that it was also just the time they were living in. This family would certainly have been considered radically progressive by the standards of their time.

One of my favorite scenes of this show was the scene where Muhammad dropped the metal pot. His teaching to Sitara had a ring of truth to it. With wisdom comes true power after all. This is a recurring message throughout the show, that knowledge and wisdom are the most important things to seek. After all by the end of the season the characters that still remain in power are the ones that used their intellect to triumph. I wonder going forward, just who will be the "witch of Mongolia"? I am not entirely convinced that it is the main character Sitara, although I think that is the highest likelihood. I really hope there will be a season two, or I will have to riot.

Anyway I highly recommend this show if you like a more intellectual anime with beautiful animation and a love for human cultures.
▼A deeply touching story - Klara and the Sun by Kazuo Ishiguro (book) - 9/12/26
Klara and the Sun is one of those rare stories that manages to get me more in touch with my self. The main character is not a human, but some kind of android. Yet I felt myself relating to her on a very deep level. I was particularly fascinated by how Klara perceived reality. This was something that was never explained from a 3rd person perspective, yet what klara described from her experience of her surroundings is reminiscent of my own experience of schizophrenia, and how it effects my perception of the world. I am assuming that some of what the reader or an outside observer would find bizarre about her experience can be explained by the fact that she is so fresh to the world. I wonder if we could give a newborn baby the ability to verbally communicate its experience of reality, it would not be similarly strange. She has not had a chance to ground her experience in a cultural/social context like humans probably do over time, leading to humans mostly communicating about reality in a very similar way. Even considering this though, there still seemed to be some ways she saw the world that were strange in an unaccounted way. This was never fully explained to the reader and one can only use their imagination as to what it meant.

I cannot honestly say if I thought this book was a happy or a sad book. In the end Josie seemed to have her miracle, seemingly brought about by Klara's faith, but what can be said for Klara? I found myself anxious a lot in the book that something bad would happen to Klara. She had feelings after all: fear, sadness, etc. Yet in the end she did not seem to be unhappy even though she was alone, fading slowly in a scrapyard. In the course of her life she understood love, and even had some experience of receiving affection. But in the end, I wished that Klara had been loved more in her life. One of the questions that I feel this book touches on are the ethics of creating a conscious android-like machine. In my opinion, an actual being has to experience being this created "tool". How could we, having any kind of heart, subject something to that kind of life?

Another big philosophical topic that this book touched on was the existence of a heart in a more spiritual context. In this book science seems to have come to some kind of final conclusion as to the nature of consciousness, so it is deemed to be irrational to believe in such a thing as a heart. It seems to me that nothing blinds humans more than being sure they know how things work. A good example of this is the man who was making the "portrait". What a miserable way to see the world, if you thing that someone can be replaced and duplicated so easily, all because he was convinced he knew how the world worked based on scientific evidence that will probably be replaced by a new discovery in so many years. Josie's father and Klara, however, kept faith in the heart.

I also wanted to comment on the way faith was portrayed in this story, because it lead to some interesting insights. To others, what Klara believed in could have been reduced to "AF superstition". To them the Sun is just a ball of gas way out in space. Yet to Klara, the Sun in something much more. And in the end, didn't that so called ball of gas bring about a miracle? Perhaps, perhaps not. But it is another point how being convinced of the way things are through some kind of proof or evidence can blind you to truth. The humans would never have thought to put their faith in something that they were convinced to be mere material. And if not for Klara, there might have been no hope. This also ties back again into the belief in the "heart" that was presented in the story as well. Those that saw nothing there did not open a door to something greater.

I am glad that I read this book, and somehow it helped me gain greater compassion for myself. The more compassion I have for myself, the more I am able to give and receive love. What more could I ask from a book? I will definitely be checking out the other works by this author.
▼Timeless wisdom, but left unsatisfied - The Alchemist by Paulo Coelho (book) - 9/3/26
while I was ultimately left unsatisfied by this book, I would still strongly suggest reading it. There is much wisdom present in these pages and it is still a fun story. Why was i unsatisfied? well I guess I wish there was more story here. It was rather short, which is not necessarily a bad thing, but mainly I felt like some of the story itself was sacrificed for the sake of presenting philosophy. I was just left wanting more from it even though it said what it wanted to say. The philosophy presented is a bit of a unique spin on the general essence of classic spiritual teachings, although I am not familiar with alchemy so it may be more accurate to that interpretation of truth. It was certainly inspiring to me to not give up searching for my own journey in life, and to take opportunities when they come. I can't ever let myself think that I am unable to live life to the fullest, or go on an adventure. We all can pursue our "personal legends". All in all it was still a good book, and I would love to read more fiction that incorporates some kind of spiritual teaching into it. I just wish there had been more to the story itself.
▼An amazing series - Rascal does not Dream series (anime) - 9/2/2026
This review is from season 1 to knapsack girl. I had seen season 1 and the first movie, Dreaming girl, some years ago when they first came out. In the last few days I decided to binge the whole series again so i could watch the new content.

Where should i start with the review? Probably with the little sister character. This series is, in my opinion, a very good and compassionate portrayal of mental illness. As someone who has really struggled with mental illness for most of my life, I feel like there are different parts of each main character that I can relate to. Mostly though, and most recently in my life, I feel like i can relate to the little sister character. Her struggle to get back into life made me feel very seen. Sometimes it is indeed almost too much to do something basic like talking to others or stepping out the door. Most days i feel like I am carrying around a crushing weight on my chest that prevents me from doing basic things in life, not to mention even having relationships with others. Seeing how the sister tried so hard to not remain trapped in her current place reminded me that I don't need to be trapped either. Other characters like Sakuta's mom are also relatable. I am fortunate enough to not have been in and out of a mental hospital often, but her struggle is very real. All of the character's struggles with mental health are not shown in a way that makes them look bad, or tells the viewer to judge them. Their suffering is shown in a very compassionate and human way and I hope that it helps raise awareness about mental illness in general.

Besides the good coverage of serious topics, this show is also just fun. There is quite a few tropes and anime in-culture, but it is well written and does not detract from the show, in my opinion as a seasoned otaku. I would probably be hesitant to suggest this show to a complete normie, but if you are a well cultured fan this is a must watch. I also love the characters. They all feel like real people. I can't stand shows where the main character is just a "loser" or a "studly stud" stereotype, I like characters with real depth, like how real people are anyway. Sakuta is not exactly Mr. popular, but he has a good heart and treats people well, and he also has a fun sense of humor. He's not one layered and I appreciate that.

Finally, I want to touch on the romance. It is super romantic!! I am a sucker for good romance. I do just wish that Sakuta and Mai would hurry up and kiss already though! But I appreciate the mature slow progress. The way that Sakuta kept himself up for days so he would not forget Mai, and then following his remembrance of her with his yelled confession to the whole school, was one of the most romantic scenes i have ever seen in anime. When I just watched the Knapsack Girl movie, I thought it was super cute when their roles were reversed. I also have to salute Sakuta in being a person of culture and liking being bullied by Mai (He's just like me for real). One thing i really wanted to see was Sakuta introducing Mai to his parents for the first time. I hope they actually show that at some point and don't gloss over it. I am excited to see where their relationship goes. I mean there was practically a marriage proposal in Knapsack Girl with Mai telling Sakuta they should become a family, but they haven't even kissed on screen! Well i hope it all pays off in the end!!!

Anyway I highly recommend this show to any long time anime fans and those that appreciate a good romance. It feels like it should be masochistic for me, as someone who is getting into their 30's without ever having had a relationship, to love romance anime, but somehow it actually helps heal my hurt heart?
