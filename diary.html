▼Went to the county fair yesterday - 9/15/2026
The fair was... okay? I went with my grandparents on free senior day so there was crowds of elderly people. The biggest disappointment was the food! I always have in my head an idea that fair food is unhealthy, but tasty-greasy street food. In reality it is just kind of low quality and completely mediocre. AND unhealthy. There was some good huckleberry ice cream though. The poor animals were very cute. I felt like Harry Potter looking at the snake in the zoo the whole time. I couldn't really enjoy myself looking at beings being treated like that. I just have too much compassion. Watching people lean in close to the animals making annoying noises at them while they were in a tiny pen was cringe worthy. I know that I am a bit hypocritical as I still eat meat, but at least I can acknowledge the harm being done and not make a spectacle about it. They even had a beef cuts poster hanging above the cute fuzzy cows showing all the different cuts of beef that one could get from them.

As one could expect from the Spokane fair, it was a very politically homogenous crowd. Expect trump posters and Christian nationalism. I have no problem with Christianity itself, but slapping trump on a bible with big guns is not where it's at. My grandparents are unfortunately in this crowd. I still maintain a relationship with them but I have to ask myself, how much is too much? I think as long as they keep their politics away from me I can maintain a sense of peace with them, but even then it still makes me feel queasy knowing the kind of things they believe in. Not to mention that sometimes they just can't help themselves and say things that make me uncomfortable. I think I really have to ask myself if maintaining a relationship with them is worth it. I think the only reason I maintain a relationship with them at all is that I have a lot of wonderful childhood memories of being with them, so it is hard. I think at least I need to just only see them once in a while because it's a bit too much. At the end of the day when dealing with people in these situations, hatred is not the solution. Hatred does not yield to further hatred after all. When we are free from hatred we can see through to a more wise solution.
The one thing about the fair that I do like is seeing all of the local arts and crafts. There are some really good paintings and photography on display which is fun to see, and I think it's fun to find all of the otaku representation haha.
Looking back at what I wrote, Maybe the fair is not something that I really need to go to anymore. There is not much for me there.... If going to local events is what I want to do, then I should keep an eye out for something that more suits my interests. I know the local Buddhist temples sometimes have things going on, and even if I don't always agree with some Buddhist dogma, I think I will still enjoy events there. There is also a local Hindu temple that sometimes does events... Homework for me to do!

▼On sobriety - 9/7/2026
I have been completely sober from all substances, including caffeine, for about a month now. Caffeine was the last one to go, and I only quit it at all because I realized it was just another chronic stimulus on my brain/body that I do not need at the moment. Since cutting out caffeine my overall anxiety has gotten less. Since being completely sober, I seem to be able to make better spiritual progress in mediation, bhakti, and fearlessness. I have not been really addicted to anything since I quit smoking cigarettes but I was occasionally messing with alcohol and cannabis, and neither of those things does me well, especially cannabis. Cannabis is something that I really should not be doing, as often it can make me afraid and have terrifying hallucinations. Sometimes it does make me feel good though, and for some reason I find myself having strong cravings for it once in a while.
I think once I fully understand that for whatever pleasure it brings me, the bad side of it is too great for me to ever mess with it, then I should be able to stay away. It can often just be too intense of an experience and leave me traumatized and existentially afraid.

I think the only thing I would really say is a "problem" with being completely sober is a kind of mental boredom, or wanting to have a higher amount of mental stimulation. This boredom is an excellent drive to progress spiritually though! I do sometimes find myself wanting something to take for a quick fix for depressed feelings, but I also see depression as a call to make spiritual progress as well. Certainly one can numb the feelings or find a lazy fix, but you can also truly heal from your wounds and move to a better place without having to rely on all that stuff.
Thankfully I always deeply understood that substances never really made me happy, even if I wanted them to. In fact they often made me feel worse and worse. Anyway here's to being sober and making genuine progress! And here is to fully understanding that whatever good feelings such things may bring, the harm they also bring is great as well, and true love is to be found without them.
▼Storytime: my experience hitchhiking - part one - 9/6/2026
I am probably one of the few people in my generation or younger that has extensively hitchhiked. I hitchhiked from Spokane Washington all the way down to near Los Angeles. I had run away from home because I did not want to be committed to a metal hospital; my family had surprised me with an intervention style meeting, and I excused myself out of the window of my room instead of going with them. I actually started my journey by hopping on a train from the valley into the downtown area, because my family found me and tried to stop a bus driver from letting me on. From there I walked up the highway a bit until there was a good spot for me to stick up my thumb like I had always heard about.
I am very lucky overall that I only had as much trouble as I did from the people I hitched with, and I would not recommend that anyone EVER hitchhike unless they have no other option. Things could have been much worse.

The first guy that gave me a ride was a trucker, he only took me a small distance and it was so uneventful that I don't remember much about him. My next ride was with a guy in a truck, and he took me all the way to Tacoma. I can't remember if he had a dog or not, but he was a cool older gentleman, and wasted no time telling me all about how he grew magic mushrooms under his sink in jars!
During my whole trip I did also spend some time on local buses, and I managed to catch the bus from Tacoma to Seattle, and then Seattle to the Forks Area, then to the first city in Oregon, where I would later start hitching down the 101. I think my first hitch in Oregon was a cool older guy in a red convertible that had some fun conversations with me. Around the Tillamook area I decided to head inland to Portland, annnd this is where I met my first "creeper". He picked me up and after a little while on the freeway he started trying to grab my leg, I kept pushing his hand away, and he eventually gave up, but I think he started touching himself and it was really gross. This continued on until we got into Portland, and I was relieved when he actually let me out to continue on my very disgusted way. It took me a couple days to get that out of my head.

I should also probably mention that I am physically male and looked like I could put up a big fight, which probably protected me a lot, but I was certainly not immune!
After hanging out in Portland for a bit and getting thrown out of a few strip clubs because I didn't have any money and also probably stank, I had a pit stop in Salem where I met a cool redheaded guy who had a tattoo of a scorpion. He taught me how to panhandle at a local Mc'Donalds with a cardboard sign and we split the cash. He also showed me to the homeless shelter there and got me a sleeping bag. Thanks dude.
I would not recommend hitchhiking out of Salem; it was very difficult. I eventually got a ride over to the coast where a strange, yet kind younger woman picked me up by herself and brought me back to her place. She fed me and gave me a backpack and pointed me to the nearest bus stop. I am grateful but thinking back on it I am worried that she was just picking up random men on the side of the road when she was all alone. I hope she is doing okay.
I don't have much to say on my journey down from there into San Francisco, but there were a bunch of people that kept offering me ganja to smoke, and I enjoyed that. I also remember finding a dumpster full of fresh doughnuts behind a doughnut shop. That was certainly one of the highlights of my trip!

Once I got to San Francisco I actually ended up crashing there for a very long time. I have no idea how long, but it was some months if I am not mistaken. When I first got into the city I made my way to Haight Street. I had never heard of Haight street so I don't know what drove me there. Stopping in Buena Vista park, there was a group of homeless youths hanging out by the stairs. I think I stopped and sat by them, and their leader, a redhead whose name had something to do with scorpion or scorpio, gave me some acid. There were many such occasions where he would come by me randomly and drop acid in my mouth.
It was not until later that I realized that there was a weird coincidence that I met two redheaded men who had something to do with scorpions, but thinking back on it now it was kind of weird.
